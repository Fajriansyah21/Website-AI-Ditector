# Annotation Guidelines v1.0

**Status:** Proposed for pilot use; revise after double-annotation and adjudication.  
**Language:** Bahasa Indonesia.  
**Unit:** source spans, sentences, documents.

## 1. Purpose

Annotate the known production history of text, not how “AI-like” it sounds. Fluency, grammar, punctuation, formality, topic, and model detector scores are not evidence of provenance by themselves.

## 2. Labels

### HUMAN
Use when the sentence's substantive wording and ideas are written by a human and there is no known substantive AI contribution to that sentence.

Examples:
- A participant writes an answer from their own knowledge without AI assistance.
- A human writes a sentence and only uses a spelling checker or mechanical punctuation correction, with no substantive rewrite.
- A human fully rewrites an AI draft into their own independently composed sentence. Do not automatically label this MIXED merely because an AI draft existed earlier; evaluate whether the final sentence retains substantive AI-generated content or phrasing.

### AI
Use when the sentence is generated by an AI system and has no substantive human editing. Copying the output into a document or changing formatting alone does not make it MIXED.

Examples:
- An AI model generates a paragraph and it is kept verbatim.
- A human accepts AI output without changing its substantive wording or content.
- Only whitespace or formatting is changed.

### MIXED
Use when substantive human and AI contributions both remain in the final sentence.

Examples:
- A human changes the claim, adds a new reason, and retains AI-generated supporting text in the same sentence.
- A human merges their own substantive clause with an AI-generated clause.
- AI generates a sentence and the human meaningfully restructures or edits it while retaining identifiable AI contribution.

### UNCERTAIN
Use when the provenance evidence is insufficient or contradictory, or the annotator cannot reasonably determine whether a substantive contribution came from a human or AI.

Examples:
- A public web text has no reliable authorship or editing history.
- A participant cannot recall whether AI generated or rewrote the submitted passage.
- The text has been copied through multiple unknown sources.
- The annotator only has style-based suspicion.

UNCERTAIN is an audit/abstention status, not one of the three primary model classes. Never force it into HUMAN, AI, or MIXED to balance classes.

## 3. What counts as substantive?

A change is generally substantive if it changes meaning, claims, reasoning, organization, or meaningful wording. Examples:
- adding/removing a claim or supporting reason;
- changing a conclusion or interpretation;
- rewriting sentence structure or phrasing beyond mechanical correction;
- merging independently written content with generated content;
- reorganizing argument flow.

A change is generally mechanical if it fixes a typo, spacing, capitalization, or punctuation without changing meaning or substantive phrasing.

Borderline changes require annotator notes and, where relevant, adjudication. Do not use a universal “percentage of words changed” rule as the sole label criterion.

## 4. Span-level procedure

1. Read the available provenance record and editing history before assigning labels.
2. Identify spans whose origin is supported by evidence: human, AI, or unknown.
3. Mark the provenance source: participant declaration, generation log, version diff, source revision, or other documented evidence.
4. If provenance is inferred only from style, mark it unknown.
5. Retain offsets to the exact raw text using zero-based, half-open Unicode code-point indices.
6. Do not overwrite raw text to make annotation easier.

## 5. Sentence-level derivation

- Only substantive human contribution, with no known substantive AI contribution → HUMAN.
- Only AI-generated substantive contribution, with no substantive human editing → AI.
- Substantive contribution from both sources remains in the sentence → MIXED.
- Insufficient or conflicting evidence → UNCERTAIN.

A sentence with both human and AI spans is not automatically MIXED if the earlier AI material was completely replaced and no substantive AI contribution remains in the final sentence. Record the history; label the final text under the policy above.

Sentence boundaries must be generated by a consistent segmentation version. Store the segmenter/version. If a sentence is truncated, malformed, or cannot be segmented reliably, flag it for review rather than silently changing offsets.

## 6. Document-level label

Document labels are derived summaries, not substitutes for sentence labels:
- HUMAN: all eligible sentences are HUMAN.
- AI: all eligible sentences are AI.
- MIXED: at least one substantive HUMAN and one substantive AI contribution remain in the document, or at least one sentence is MIXED.
- UNCERTAIN: provenance is insufficient for a defensible document-level label.

If a document contains a mix of HUMAN and AI sentences but no individual MIXED sentence, document label is MIXED. If uncertain sentences materially affect the document-level decision, use UNCERTAIN or a documented mixed/uncertain status outside the three-class target. Do not hide uncertainty.

## 7. Adjudication examples

| Case | Label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Human writes original response without AI | HUMAN | Provenance confirmed |
| AI writes paragraph, copied without substantive edits | AI | No substantive human contribution |
| AI draft, human corrects only typos | AI | Mechanical edits do not change provenance |
| AI draft, human adds a new claim and rewrites a sentence while retaining generated content | MIXED | Both contributions remain |
| Human completely rewrites AI draft in their own words, no substantive AI wording/content retained | HUMAN for final sentence if supported by version history | Prior exposure alone is not enough to label final sentence MIXED |
| Public article with unknown AI-editing history | UNCERTAIN | Public access is not provenance evidence |
| Contributor says “I wrote this” but cannot clarify use of AI when asked and records conflict | UNCERTAIN pending adjudication | Evidence conflicts |
| Human and AI responses answer the same prompt but are independent | Label each by its own origin; shared pair/group for split | Pairing is not shared authorship |
| A human adds punctuation and changes line breaks to raw AI text | AI | Formatting only |
| AI expands a human outline into prose and human keeps some generated sentences and writes others | Sentence labels may differ; document likely MIXED | Annotate at sentence/span level |

## 8. Annotator workflow

For each document:
- review provenance and source approval;
- review version history;
- annotate span provenance;
- assign sentence labels;
- assign document label;
- select confidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW;
- enter evidence source and rationale for any MIXED, UNCERTAIN, or disputed case;
- flag privacy, copyright, duplicate, or source concerns;
- do not consult detector predictions during annotation.

Double-annotate at least 20% of the pilot. At least two annotators label the same subset independently before seeing each other's labels. Adjudicate all disagreements in that subset. Report agreement with class counts; Cohen's Kappa may be included, but do not treat a single score as proof of label validity, especially with class imbalance.

## 9. Quality flags

Recommended flags:
- `PROVENANCE_MISSING`
- `CONSENT_MISSING`
- `LICENSE_PENDING`
- `AI_HISTORY_UNKNOWN`
- `VERSION_CONFLICT`
- `POSSIBLE_DUPLICATE`
- `OFFSET_MISMATCH`
- `PRIVATE_OR_SENSITIVE_CONTENT`
- `COPYRIGHT_CONCERN`
- `SEGMENTATION_UNCERTAIN`

A critical flag prevents training admission until resolved.

## 10. Adjudication record

Record:
- document_id, sentence_id/span_id;
- initial labels from each annotator;
- final label;
- evidence reviewed;
- reason for decision;
- adjudicator pseudonymous ID;
- timestamp and guideline version.

Do not erase initial labels. Preserve the audit trail.
